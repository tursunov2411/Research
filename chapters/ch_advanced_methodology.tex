\section{Data, Research Design, and Methodology}

\subsection{Research Philosophy and Ontological Position}

This dissertation adopts a \textit{critical realist} ontological position \citep{bhaskar1975}. Critical realism holds that social phenomena are produced by underlying causal mechanisms operating within stratified structures, and that observed empirical regularities are surface manifestations of these deeper generative processes. For the purposes of this study, the structural mechanisms of interest are: (i) global value chain governance, which allocates productive tasks across national boundaries through firm-level coordination decisions; (ii) the political economy of developmental states, which shapes domestic institutional configurations that make countries more or less receptive to GVC embedding; and (iii) the geography of landlocked developing countries, which imposes structural trade cost penalties that condition the ceiling on achievable integration.

This ontological position has methodological consequences. It motivates a mixed-method research design that combines quantitative empirical analysis --- to detect regularities and test hypotheses --- with qualitative-comparative analysis --- to trace mechanisms and interpret regularities in their structural context. A purely econometric approach would identify correlations but could not answer \textit{why} Uzbekistan's reform trajectory has diverged from Vietnam's, or \textit{through which mechanisms} Northeast Asian FDI translates (or fails to translate) into domestic capability accumulation. A purely case-study approach would identify mechanisms but could not assess their magnitude or generalisability.

Epistemologically, this dissertation follows a \textit{retroductive} inference strategy: it begins with observed empirical anomalies (e.g., the gap between Uzbekistan's rapidly rising intermediate goods import share and its stagnant export sophistication), identifies candidate causal mechanisms (captive GVC governance, logistics constraints, FDI quality gaps), and evaluates the plausibility of those mechanisms against systematic empirical evidence \citep{danermark2019}. This approach is well-suited to development economics research where controlled experimentation is impossible and causal identification is inherently partial.

\subsection{Research Design: Sequential Explanatory Mixed-Methods}

The empirical strategy follows a sequential explanatory design, proceeding through four stages:

\begin{enumerate}
 \item \textbf{Trend Identification:} Descriptive time-series analysis of manufacturing value added, FDI inflows, trade openness, and economic complexity for Uzbekistan and comparator countries, 2000--2023.
 \item \textbf{Structural Break Detection:} Chow test applied to the manufacturing VA series to formally identify 2017 as a statistical turning point, converting a qualitative reform claim into a quantitatively testable hypothesis.
 \item \textbf{Association Analysis:} OLS regressions relating FDI inflows, trade openness, and reform-period dummies to manufacturing upgrading indicators, with HC3-robust standard errors.
 \item \textbf{Comparative Benchmarking:} Alignment of Uzbekistan's reform trajectory against Vietnam (post-2001) and Thailand (post-Eastern Seaboard Programme, 1981) using reform-year normalisation to control for initial conditions.
\end{enumerate}

Alongside this quantitative sequence, qualitative-comparative analysis of institutional conditions, SEZ policy design, and structural constraints provides the mechanistic layer that the regressions cannot supply.

This study draws on four principal data sources. First, the World Bank World Development Indicators (WDI) database \citep{world_bank_wdi} provides annual macroeconomic indicators for Uzbekistan and comparator countries, including manufacturing value added as a share of GDP, FDI net inflows as a share of GDP, GDP per capita, trade openness, and the Logistics Performance Index. Data span 2010--2026, with 2024--2026 supplemented by IMF WEO April 2025 projections where WDI data are unavailable. The primary analytical window for historical analysis is 2010--2023.

Second, the Observatory of Economic Complexity (OEC) BACI international trade database provides bilateral merchandise trade flows at the HS-2 section level. Intermediate goods are proxied using HS sections 07 (plastics and rubbers), 15 (base metals), 16 (machinery), 17 (transportation equipment), and 18 (instruments) --- sections representing capital goods and inputs rather than final consumption. Revealed comparative advantage indices are computed using the Balassa (1965) formula:
\begin{equation}
RCA_{ik} = \frac{X_{ik}/X_i}{X_{wk}/X_w}
\label{eq:rca}
\end{equation}
where an RCA value exceeding 1 indicates a comparative advantage \citep{balassa1965}.

Third, the Economic Complexity Index is sourced from the OEC, which computes annual ECI scores using the method of reflections algorithm of \citet{hidalgo2009}. Fourth, FDI origin data are sourced from the National Statistics Committee of the Republic of Uzbekistan's 2024 report on foreign investment.

\subsection{Empirical Models}

\subsubsection{Structural Break Analysis}

To formally test whether the 2017 reform package constituted a statistically significant structural break in Uzbekistan's manufacturing trajectory, the Chow (1960) test is applied to the linear trend specification:
\begin{equation}
 MfgVA_t = \alpha + \beta \cdot \text{Trend}_t + \varepsilon_t, \quad t = 2010, \ldots, 2023
 \label{eq:chow_base}
\end{equation}
where $MfgVA_t$ is manufacturing value added as a share of GDP in year $t$. The test decomposes equation (\ref{eq:chow_base}) into sub-period regressions for $t < 2017$ and $t \geq 2017$. The F-statistic:
\begin{equation}
 F = \frac{(RSS_R - RSS_U)/k}{RSS_U/(n-2k)}
 \label{eq:chow_f}
\end{equation}
where $RSS_R$ is the residual sum of squares from the restricted pooled regression, $RSS_U = RSS_{pre} + RSS_{post}$, and $k=2$ parameters are tested. A rejection of the null hypothesis $H_0: \alpha_{pre} = \alpha_{post}, \beta_{pre} = \beta_{post}$ constitutes evidence that the 2017 reforms generated a structurally distinct growth regime for manufacturing.

\subsubsection{OLS Regression: Manufacturing Value Added and Economic Complexity}

To estimate the association between Northeast Asian economic engagement and manufacturing upgrading, the following OLS specifications are estimated:
\begin{equation}
 MfgVA_t = \alpha_0 + \beta_1 \cdot FDI_{t-1} + \beta_2 \cdot IntermShare_t + \beta_3 \cdot TradeOpen_t + \beta_4 \cdot Post2017_t + \beta_5 \cdot \text{Trend}_t + \varepsilon_t
 \label{eq:ols_mfg}
\end{equation}

A second specification tests for delayed capital-stock effects, accounting for the time between investment commitments and productive capacity coming online:
\begin{equation}
 MfgVA_t = \alpha_0 + \beta_1 \cdot FDI_{t-2} + \beta_2 \cdot IntermShare_t + \beta_3 \cdot TradeOpen_t + \beta_4 \cdot Post2017_t + \beta_5 \cdot \text{Trend}_t + \varepsilon_t
 \label{eq:ols_mfg2}
\end{equation}

For economic complexity, the specification is:
\begin{equation}
 ECI_t = \alpha_0 + \beta_1 \cdot MfgVA_t + \beta_2 \cdot TradeOpen_t + \beta_3 \cdot Post2017_t + \beta_4 \cdot \text{Trend}_t + \varepsilon_t
 \label{eq:ols_eci}
\end{equation}

All regressions use HC3 heteroskedasticity-consistent standard errors \citep{mackinnon1985}. Given the short annual time series ($N = 14$), these models are underpowered for precise coefficient estimation and are interpreted as descriptive associations rather than causal estimates.

\subsubsection{Scenario Forecasting}

Three forward scenarios are constructed for manufacturing value added through 2030. Scenario A (Status Quo) extrapolates the post-2017 linear trend with a 95\% prediction interval:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{MfgVA}_t = \hat{\alpha}_{post} + \hat{\beta}_{post} \cdot \text{Trend}_t, \quad t = 2024, \ldots, 2030
 \label{eq:forecast_base}
\end{equation}

Scenario B (Accelerated GVC Embedding) departs from (\ref{eq:forecast_base}) by adding an annual increment calibrated to the intermediate goods import share coefficient ($\beta_{interm} = 2.34$) estimated from the OLS regression:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{MfgVA}^B_t = \widehat{MfgVA}_t + \beta_{interm} \cdot (IntermShare^*_t - IntermShare_{2023})
 \label{eq:forecast_b}
\end{equation}
where $IntermShare^*_t$ is the target trajectory implied by reaching Vietnam's equivalent intermediate goods share.

Scenario C (Vietnam Trajectory) applies Vietnam's observed annual percentage-point change ($\hat{\beta}_{VNM}$) in manufacturing VA at the comparable post-reform stage (years 1--8 after Doi Moi deepening).

\subsection{Scope, Limitations, and Robustness}

Uzbekistan is not covered by the OECD Trade in Value Added (TiVA) database. The network analysis therefore uses bilateral merchandise trade flows as a proxy for production network integration, with intermediate goods shares estimated from HS section-level trade data rather than value-added decomposition. This is a recognised limitation: the betweenness centrality results reflect trade volume flows, not domestic value capture, and should be interpreted as a lower-bound proxy for GVC positioning rather than a direct measure of network embeddedness. Specifically, high intermediate import shares may overstate backward integration if a substantial fraction of imported intermediates is consumed in non-export-oriented domestic construction and infrastructure rather than in manufacturing for re-export. Conversely, the absence of value-added data means that any domestic value added to re-exported goods is not captured, potentially understating forward integration. These caveats reinforce the interpretation throughout this dissertation that the trade-based GVC metrics are \textit{proxy indicators} whose directional trends are informative even if their absolute magnitudes should not be treated as precise measures of production network embeddedness.

This dissertation faces three principal limitations that should be acknowledged at the outset. First, the primary Uzbekistan time series spans only 2010--2023 ($N=14$), constraining statistical power in OLS models. The Chow test requires a minimum of four observations per sub-period for stable F-statistics, which is marginally satisfied but leaves limited degrees of freedom. Robustness checks using alternative breakpoints (2016, 2018) are reported in the Appendix.

Second, the trade data rely on reported mirror statistics from OEC/BACI, which are known to contain measurement error from misclassification, informal trade, and gold transit flows. Uzbekistan's large informal gold trade is partially recorded as ``arts and antiques'' in official statistics, which affects RCA calculations for this sector. All RCA values for HS section 14 should be interpreted with this caveat.

Third, the comparative analysis normalises reform trajectories to a common ``year zero,'' abstracting from differences in initial conditions (coastal access, population size, institutional heritage) that may independently explain divergent outcomes. The comparative benchmarking is therefore illustrative rather than causal.

Despite these limitations, the combination of structural break testing, bilateral trade decomposition, comparative benchmarking, and institutional analysis provides a methodologically coherent basis for the policy conclusions advanced in Section~\ref{sec:policy}.
